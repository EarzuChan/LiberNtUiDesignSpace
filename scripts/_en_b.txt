683|Controls
684|Basic controls keep Fluent’s structure and interaction model. Controls have “thickness”: the bottom edge is darker in light mode and the top edge is lighter in dark mode. Hover and pressed states change only the fill, never the size. Everything below is live.
685|Buttons
686|Use one accent button per view. Subtle buttons are for toolbars and secondary actions (circular when icon-only); danger buttons appear only in confirmation dialogs. Form buttons use a small 4px radius and capsules are reserved for standalone actions. Pressing scales the button to 0.98.
687|Selection
688|Switches are for settings that apply immediately, checkboxes for multiple choices that are submitted, radio buttons for mutually exclusive options. The checkbox is circular: the tick is drawn from inside the circle out past its edge, using the on-accent color inside and flipping to the accent color outside, so it reads differently from a radio button’s dot at a glance. When off, the switch is an outlined track with a small dot; when on, the knob grows and slides across on a spring curve.
689|Input
690|A focused text field shows a 2px accent line along its bottom edge. Search boxes are always capsules and use an outer focus ring instead. The fat-track slider is a capsule too. The dot inside a slider thumb grows on hover and shrinks when pressed.
691|Navigation
692|The selected item is marked by a short 3×16 accent bar on the left and switches to the Filled icon. Segmented controls are capsules, and the selection block slides between options.

695|Content first
696|The shell steps back. Window bases, the dock and flyouts all defer to the wallpaper and content, keeping only the hint of form they need.
697|Grounded materials
698|A glass surface’s tint lightness and opacity adapt to the luminance behind it. Foreground colors follow only the system light/dark mode, so legibility stays stable.
699|Clear hierarchy
700|Blur, soft shadows and hairlines express depth, not heavy borders. Edges get an inner highlight only, with no refraction.
701|Restrained motion
702|Enter fast and settle gently; exit promptly. Spring curves are used only on objects you manipulate directly, such as dock icons and switches.
703|The visual language of LiberNT: Fluent Design’s structure and control system as the skeleton, with a more translucent, more restrained adaptive glass material. This page collects the colors, type, metrics, materials, icons and basic controls.
704|Cyan
705|All colors are defined in OKLCH: lightness is perceptually uniform and hue does not drift as lightness changes. The accent color is extracted from the wallpaper by default, and solid surfaces carry a hint of that hue. The idea is borrowed from Material You, but Fluent’s single-accent system is kept: there are no secondary or tertiary palettes.
706|Wallpaper color extraction
707|The wallpaper is downsampled and every pixel is converted to OKLCH, then bucketed by hue (15° per bucket). Each bucket scores as area share
708|\s× mean chroma, so hues that are both large and vivid win. Up to 4 candidates are kept, at least 40° apart in hue. Pixels that are too dark, too bright or too gray are ignored. The day and night images of a dynamic wallpaper are sampled separately, and a near-monochrome wallpaper yields a low-chroma accent rather than an invented color. See\s
709|Guardrails: chroma is clamped to 0.085–0.19. Yellow and lime hues (h≈40–150) get a higher base lightness in light mode, and text on them switches to a dark color automatically (
710|The accent hue is also fed into neutral surfaces: the solid window fallback, content panes, cards and the tint layer of glass. There are three strengths: Off (pure neutral), Soft (default) and Vivid. In dark mode cards use tonal elevation: higher layers are lighter and more tinted, relying less on shadow. A low-chroma accent weakens the tint automatically.

753|Materials
754|Shell surfaces use adaptive glass: the content behind is blurred and its saturation raised, then a tint is laid on top. The tint is not a fixed value. It is adjusted in real time from the average luminance behind the surface, so the same piece of glass stays both translucent and legible over bright and dark backdrops.
755|Thickness
756|The five thicknesses share one tint formula and differ only in blur radius and the opacity factor k. Surfaces closer to the user and carrying more text are thicker.
757|Adaptive tint
758|\sis the average luminance behind the surface (0 dark → 1 bright). In light mode, the darker the backdrop, the more solid the white tint, to hold dark text. Dark mode is the reverse: the brighter the backdrop, the more solid the dark tint. Over a very dark backdrop the tint instead gets lighter and thinner, so the glass lifts off the background. Foreground colors always follow only the system light/dark mode.
759|Tint formula
760|L = l0 + (l1 − l0) × bl          /* tint lightness */\nA = (a0 + (a1 − a0) × bl) × k    /* opacity, capped at 0.97 */\n\nbackground: oklch(L c h / A);     /* h and c come from the accent (surface tint) */\nbackdrop-filter: blur(r) saturate(s);\ntransition: --bl 420ms linear;   /* registered as <number> via @property */
761|Luminance sampling
762|The wallpaper is downsampled to a 64×36 luminance grid, and windows take part as occluders in z-order. Each glass surface averages up to 10×10 sample points inside its own rectangle: the desktop layer sees only the wallpaper, the window layer sees the wallpaper and lower windows, and the shell layer sees everything. Sampling converts coordinates according to the wallpaper fit (fill / fit / stretch / center / tile) and accounts for the dark-mode dimming. It is recomputed when windows move or the theme or wallpaper changes, and the result is written to\s

764|Wallpaper dimming in dark mode
765|In dark mode an overly bright wallpaper makes the whole desktop glare and turns dark glass gray. The system dims adaptively from the wallpaper’s average luminance: nothing below 0.32, more for brighter images, capped at 50% (
766|). The default overlay is neutral black, which leaves the photo’s hues alone. It can be switched to Tinted, which overlays a very dark shade of the accent hue for a more unified mood at the cost of a color cast. The dimming amount is shared with the luminance sampler, and the lock screen uses the same layer.

904|Radius is split into two extremes by role rather than growing with size: structural surfaces use small radii and small pokeable controls are fully round. The 10–14 middle range is mostly avoided, because an in-between radius looks the cheapest.
